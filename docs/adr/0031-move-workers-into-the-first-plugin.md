Later changes: [ADR-0034](0034-build-plugins-on-a-plugin-sdk.md) refines decisions 1 and 4: the link's worker side moves into `mannie-link-worker`, and the executor contract moves into the plugin SDK.

## Context

- The owner made the worker system the first plugin ([decision D26](../roadmap/milestones.md#5-decisions)), and under [ADR-0030](0030-split-mannie-into-a-core-and-plugins.md) the core neither depends on a plugin nor knows one.
- M4 built workers into the core's crates. `mannie-link`, with both sides of the link, and the worker service in `apps/worker` belong to workers alone, and only the two composition roots depend on `mannie-link`. Inside the core:
  - The runtime defines the port `Delegation`, which lists the paired workers and carries submissions, task states, leases, deletions, reads of task conversations and approval decisions ([ADR-0027](0027-delegate-tasks-to-workers-through-a-runtime-port.md)). It also holds the followers of each worker, `delegate_task` with its texts, and the workers' section at position 310 among its built-in sections.
  - The runtime defines the worker's side as well: the definition `worker`, its instruction sections and its texts, which the worker service picks with `AgentRegistry::for_worker()` and `InstructionRegistry::for_worker()`, and `Machine::Worker` with the host's details, on which the runtime branches for its task slots, the owner's instructions, the workspace and the ceiling ([ADR-0028](0028-run-assigned-tasks-under-the-host-ceiling.md)).
  - The workers' repository and tables (`workers_paired`, `workers_deletions`) are a module of `mannie-store`, and the runtime's deletions call into it. The runtime's tables carry the workers' columns: migration `0017` added a task's worker, its leased profile, the version of its last state, its submission and a queued cancel to `runtime_tasks`, and `0018` a worker to `runtime_conversations`.
  - The core's contracts name workers: a task's worker and whether it was let go, a task conversation's worker, the refusals `worker_offline`, `worker_unknown`, `worker_incompatible` and `profile_not_reachable`, the errors `worker_unreachable` of chat and of tools and `full_access_refused`, and a count of workers in the activity `waiting_for_tasks`. The task parts stored since 0.2.0 hold their worker.
  - ADR-0027 decision 9 and ADR-0028 decision 7 changed other features directly. Child agents' task card and call note, chat's conversation pane, side panel, read-only footer, status line and errors, and the tools' approvals branch on workers, and the workers' texts sit under the `chat_`, `child_agents_` and `tools_` message prefixes.
  - The desktop app's composition root starts the link's hub before the runtime, and a hub that cannot read the paired workers keeps the whole core from starting.
- Workers brought mechanisms to the core that do not depend on workers: tasks that another machine assigns, with a credential that can arrive after the task; the ceiling with its refusals; a vault that tells a run when its credential arrives; and scoped redaction (ADR-0028 decisions 1 and 4 to 6, [ADR-0029](0029-let-the-owner-give-conversations-and-hosts-full-access.md)).
- A worker of 0.2.0 has to keep working with Mannie 0.3.0, one minor version behind (NFR-OPS-03), so the link's contracts stay as they are. Contracts and stored data change only by addition, and a destructive change takes two releases ([ADR-0002](0002-grow-through-extension-points.md), [ADR-0009](0009-persist-state-in-sqlite.md)).

## Decision

1. Workers is the plugin `workers`. It holds the link and its contracts; the master's side of workers, with the registry of paired workers, pairing, the link's client, delegation and its followers, credential leases, the workers' section and the logic of the workers' IPC commands; and the worker service, with its configuration, host facts, host instructions, the configuration of its ceiling, the definition `worker`, its instruction sections and the texts that the worker agent reads. Its folder `plugins/workers/` holds `crates/mannie-link`, `crates/mannie-workers` for the master's side and the plugin's definition, `worker/` for the `mannie-worker` service, and `proto/`. The names of the crates, of the service and of the link's packages (`mannie.link.<service>.v1`) stay, so workers of 0.2.0 keep working and update as before.
2. These leave the core's crates for the plugin: from the runtime, the delegation port and its followers, the run side of `delegate_task`, the workers' section, the definition `worker` with its sections and texts, and the test harnesses of delegation; from the types, the workers' IPC and view types; from the store, the `workers` module with its repository and migrations; from the tools, the discovery of host facts; and from the desktop app's composition root, the hub and its key store, which become the plugin's start and its use of the credential store's service ([ADR-0030](0030-split-mannie-into-a-core-and-plugins.md) decision 13).
3. The core keeps the mechanisms that workers brought, and its documents describe them for any composition root: assigned tasks, with a credential that can arrive after its task, the activity `waiting_for_lease` and the failure `lease_revoked`; the ceiling, with its modes, decisions and refusals; the vault that tells a run when its credential arrives; scoped redaction; and conversations that an executor serves (decision 4). `Machine::Worker` becomes three settings that any composition root can give the runtime: a ceiling for its tools, a description of its machine for the instruction sections, and the number of its task slots. The runtime's branches on the worker's host become branches on those settings.
4. Task executors replace the delegation port as an extension point of the runtime. The runtime keeps the task contract of [ADR-0024](0024-run-child-agent-tasks-in-the-parent-run.md): the `task` part, numbers, the board, lanes, background tasks, `follow_up_task`, `wait_for_tasks` and recovery. Child agents are the runtime's own executor. A plugin registers an executor with:
   - its task tool: the name, the description, the strict schema, when the runtime offers it, and its refusals as codes with arguments;
   - submitting, looking up, cancelling and following its tasks, by IDs that the runtime chooses and stores first (ADR-0027 decision 3), with a version on each state so that an older state changes nothing (ADR-0027 decision 5);
   - serving its tasks' conversations: the subscription, earlier turns, the checkpoint, archived outputs, approval decisions, the automatic Allow once for a parent conversation in full access (ADR-0029 decision 5), and Stop;
   - whether its tasks outlive the reply that started them, as a worker's do (ADR-0027 decision 6), in which case the runtime lets them go when the reply ends or stops, and when the plugin stops;
   - an instruction section for the runs that are offered its tool.
5. `delegate_task` becomes the tool of the Workers plugin's executor, offered while a worker is paired. Its name, schema and texts stay, so the model sees the same tool and the same list of workers.
6. A task's part, its row and its conversation's summary name their executor in fields of the core: the kind, which is `child` or a plugin's ID, and a target with an ID and the name that the owner sees, such as the worker's. An executor's refusals and failures, and its errors in chat and in tools, reach the UI as codes with arguments, and the activity `waiting_for_tasks` counts the tasks of each kind. The fields and variants that name workers are still read and written through 0.3.0 and go in the release after.
7. The UI gets a slot of task kinds ([ADR-0020](0020-complete-the-ui-shell-slots.md) grows). For its executor's kind, a plugin registers the icon, the target's label, and the messages that the task card, the call note, a task conversation's read-only footer, the status line and the refusals and failures use. Child agents' and chat's components use the slot and lose their branches on workers, and without an entry for a kind they show generic texts that name the target. The toolbar's "Child agents" button keeps listing the kind `child`.
8. The workers' data leaves the core's tables. M5 adds the executor's columns to `runtime_tasks` and `runtime_conversations`, and the plugin's table `workers_tasks` with what only a worker task has: the worker, the leased profile, the version of its last state, the submission and a queued cancel. M5 copies the data, converts the worker in stored task documents and summaries into the executor's fields, and stops using the old columns, which the release after drops. The assignment and the state version that a worker keeps for its assigned tasks stay in the core with assigned tasks. The store's `workers` module moves into the plugin with its tables and migrations, and the store's records of the migrations of each module (ADR-0030 decision 5) carry them over from `user_version`.
9. Deleting a conversation writes its task conversations that an executor of a plugin serves into an outbox of the core, in the deletion's transaction. The plugin takes them from there when it can deliver them: at once while it is on, and when it starts otherwise. The outbox replaces the store's call into the workers' module and the table `workers_deletions`, whose rows M5 copies into it, and removing a worker drops the worker's rows from it.
10. Turning Workers off closes every link and lets each followed task go, as quitting does: its part ends with the result that the task may still run on its worker, the task keeps running there, and its approval requests wait there. Nothing is cancelled. Until Workers is on again, `delegate_task` is refused, and so are `follow_up_task` and `wait_for_tasks` for a worker task, with a result that says Workers is off. Turning Workers on connects to the paired workers and compares their tasks with the master's rows, as at startup (ADR-0027 decision 7), and `wait_for_tasks` follows a task that was let go again. When the owner has not chosen yet, Workers starts when the store holds a paired worker. A start that fails, such as one that cannot read the paired workers, leaves Workers failed and the rest of Mannie running.
11. The texts under `chat_`, `child_agents_` and `tools_` that name workers move under `workers_`, and the core's texts that stay say what happens without naming workers ([ADR-0019](0019-keep-visible-text-in-message-catalogs.md)).
12. The core's architecture documents describe the core, its extension points and plugins. The architecture of workers (workers, delegation, approvals across the link, model access on workers, pairing, the link, and compatibility and updates) moves into the Workers feature's documents. The vision and the goals describe the core and plugins, with work across machines as the first plugin.
13. This ADR refines [ADR-0015](0015-run-an-agent-runtime-on-every-machine.md), whose decisions now hold for the Workers plugin; [ADR-0016](0016-link-machines-over-pinned-mtls-grpc.md), whose link belongs to the plugin; ADR-0027, whose port becomes the plugin's executor (decision 1) and whose changes in other features become uses of the task kinds and the executor's fields (decisions 9 and 10); and ADR-0028, whose definition `worker` and its sections belong to the plugin (decisions 2 and 3) and whose changes in other features change as ADR-0027's do (decision 7).

## Consequences

- Positive: the core no longer knows workers. Delegation becomes one executor among others, so a later plugin adds a kind of task without editing the runtime. An owner without workers runs no worker code, opens no connection and sees no worker in the interface, and a fault in the workers' start no longer keeps Mannie from starting.
- Negative: removing the old columns and fields takes two releases, and contracts carry both shapes through 0.3.0. The task card and the runtime's task code go through one more indirection. About 11,000 lines of the link and the worker service move, and so do about 3,000 lines of the runtime's code and 2,000 lines of its tests, with the paths in CI, the release workflow and the contract checks.
- Risks: delegation regresses during the move. The M4 suites move with the code and must pass unchanged: the runtime's tests of delegation, then through an in-process executor, the link's tests over loopback, the crash tests and the end-to-end tests with a real worker. The data move loses or misreads a task's state, which upgrade tests from the 0.2.0 fixture database with worker tasks in every state check. Turning Workers off loses a report, which tests that turn it off at each step of a delegation check.
- Refinement in M5: decision 8 changed while the data moved. The plugin has no table of its tasks: `runtime_tasks` keeps an executor's kind, target and state version in columns of the core, and what only the executor knows, such as a worker task's leased profile, submission and queued cancel, in a document of the executor's, which the core stores as it is and never reads. A plugin's table would have to follow the core's task rows, and the core's deletions would have to reach into it. The worker's old columns stay until the release after; the table's check still asks a task without a turn for a worker, so the core writes an executor's target there as well until a later release rebuilds the table. A refusal that 0.2.0 stored keeps the text that the model read, and the core's views show the refusals and errors that name workers in the executor's shape, so the UI reads one shape (decision 6).

## Alternatives considered

| Option                                                                        | Why not chosen                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Keep the delegation port, and give it to the runtime only while Workers is on | The runtime, its texts and its contracts would still name workers, and the next plugin with a kind of task would need a port of its own |
| Keep the workers' repository in `mannie-store`                                | The core would hold a plugin's code, and the core's deletions would keep calling into it                                                |
| A separate plugin for the worker service                                      | The service is the plugin's own program and works only with the master's side                                                           |
| Move assigned tasks, the ceiling and the vault into the plugin                | They are parts of the runtime and the policy that a plugin cannot enforce from outside, and a later plugin can use them                 |
| Rename the crates and the link's packages after the plugin                    | Workers of 0.2.0 would stop working with Mannie 0.3.0                                                                                   |
| Drop the old columns in M5                                                    | ADR-0002 takes two releases for a destructive change                                                                                    |

## Validation

M5:

- The M4 suites pass from the plugin's folder: the runtime's delegation tests through an in-process executor, the link's tests over loopback, the crash tests and the end-to-end tests with a real worker.
- Tests of the executor registry with a fake executor, including one whose tasks outlive their reply, and of the task kinds slot with a fake kind and without an entry.
- Turning Workers off and on at each step of a delegation runs nothing twice and loses no report, and a failing start leaves the rest of Mannie running.
- Upgrade tests from the 0.2.0 fixture database, with worker tasks in every state, deletions waiting for an offline worker, and the move from `user_version` to records per module.
- The core's tests pass with no plugin in the build.
